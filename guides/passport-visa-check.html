Passport, visas and entry rules: a verification workflow

Use official sources to avoid the most costly planning assumption: that someone else’s entry rules apply to you.

By TravelReady Editorial · Published and reviewed 6 November 2026 · Educational information
The practical approach

Entry requirements depend on passport nationality, destination, travel purpose, duration and sometimes the transit route. Use the steps below to make a plan that fits your actual itinerary.

Describe the complete journey first

Entry requirements depend on passport nationality, destination, travel purpose, duration and sometimes the transit route. Before searching for a visa answer, write down your passport-issuing country, every country you will enter or transit, dates, purpose of travel and whether you leave the secure transit area. A change of airport, airline or connection may alter the questions you need to ask. Do not assume that a short layover or an online check-in automatically removes document requirements.

Use the right sources in the right order

Start with the government or immigration authority of your destination and relevant transit countries. Review embassy or consulate information where applicable and confirm practical boarding requirements with the operating carrier. The IATA Travel Centre is a useful cross-check, but it does not replace the relevant authorities. Search results and influencer examples may describe another traveler’s passport or purpose. Always record the official URL and the date you checked it, particularly if your itinerary is far in the future.

Read passport validity precisely

A valid passport today may still not meet a particular destination’s remaining-validity rule. Check whether the rule is measured from entry, departure or another point; requirements differ. Also inspect physical condition, available pages if relevant, the spelling of names and the match between your booking and documents. If your passport expires relatively soon, do not rely on a generic “six-month rule.” Confirm the actual country-specific wording and allow time for any necessary renewal.

Check transit as its own decision

A connection may create requirements that are not obvious from the final destination. You may need to change terminals, collect and recheck baggage, pass immigration or switch airports. Even staying airside does not universally remove transit documentation requirements. Review the entire itinerary, including separate tickets and the consequences of a delay. When a booking includes self-transfer, confirm whether you can legally and practically complete all the steps.

Keep records without exposing sensitive data

Record the document category, issuing authority, application status and verification date in your private trip notes. Avoid uploading passport scans or visa details into unfamiliar websites. A paper copy or secure offline backup can help in some emergencies, but a copy is not a substitute for an original when the authority requires one. Follow the document holder’s security needs and your country’s identity-protection advice.

Before you commit to nonrefundable costs

Verify the travel rules before paying for plans that cannot easily be changed. Recheck after substantial itinerary changes and near departure. Some permissions require advance processing; others may be linked to specific entries or dates. Do not rely on an advertising website to guarantee approval. If information conflicts across sources, contact the official authority or carrier and keep a record of the response rather than improvising at the airport.

Your action checklist

  • List nationality, destination, transit points and travel purpose.
  • Check official immigration or embassy sources and operating carrier.
  • Record the date verified and recheck after itinerary changes.

Verification and further reading

Travel conditions, airline terms, health recommendations and government rules may change. Confirm the exact requirements for your nationality, route and circumstances rather than treating this article as a live eligibility check.

  • IATA Travel Centre — check the source for current rules applicable to your travel.
