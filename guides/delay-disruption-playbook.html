When travel goes wrong: a disruption playbook

A calm sequence for cancellations, delays, missed connections and lost baggage.

By TravelReady Editorial · Published and reviewed 6 November 2026 · Educational information
The practical approach

After a delay or cancellation, use the operating carrier’s app, service desk or official channel to understand what has changed. Use the steps below to make a plan that fits your actual itinerary.

Find reliable information first

After a delay or cancellation, use the operating carrier’s app, service desk or official channel to understand what has changed. Screenshots of a social media post may be outdated. Confirm whether a new booking has been issued, whether your bags will be handled differently and whether you must take any immediate action. Keep your original booking reference and details of any replacement service. If the situation is unsafe or a medical emergency, prioritize appropriate local help.

Separate urgent and nonurgent tasks

Your first priorities may be securing the next transport option, confirming a safe place to stay, preserving access to essential medication and informing anyone expecting you. Shopping for compensation can wait until you understand your onward plan. Write down changes as they occur, especially when multiple people are traveling. If a carrier offers a rebooking option, check the full routing and timing before accepting it.

Keep evidence without obsessing over paperwork

Save booking confirmations, boarding passes, written delay notices and receipts for necessary expenses where practical. Rules about refunds, care and compensation differ by jurisdiction, carrier contract and reason for disruption. Travel insurance terms can also differ greatly. Do not treat any standard refund figure from a blog as a universal entitlement. Read the applicable official rules, carrier conditions and insurance policy before submitting a claim.

Handle baggage problems promptly

If checked baggage does not arrive, follow the carrier or airport’s official reporting process, ideally before leaving the baggage area when appropriate. Obtain a reference number and instructions for updates. Keep the luggage tag and record which essentials you need in the meantime. Do not assume the airline and insurer use the same claim process or deadlines. If you purchased essentials, retain receipts and check which expenses may be considered under the applicable conditions.

Plan communication and basic resilience

A simple message to a trusted contact should state what changed, your current location if safe to share and your next expected update. Keep the message factual. If your phone battery or network access is limited, prioritize getting the new itinerary and essential contact information first. Travelers with children or companions should agree on a safe regrouping point and who will speak with the carrier. A backup payment method can help, but protect it separately.

Review what the disruption revealed

After the trip, revisit the connection choice, document storage, packing setup and insurance assumptions. Which piece of information was hardest to reach? Which extra item really mattered? Update your personal trip checklist accordingly. The goal is not to eliminate every disruption—no planner can—but to design a journey that is easier to recover when ordinary things go wrong.

Your action checklist

  • Confirm changes through official carrier channels.
  • Keep reference numbers and necessary receipts.
  • Check policy and jurisdiction before assuming compensation.

Verification and further reading

Travel conditions, airline terms, health recommendations and government rules may change. Confirm the exact requirements for your nationality, route and circumstances rather than treating this article as a live eligibility check.
