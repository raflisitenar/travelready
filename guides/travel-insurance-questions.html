Read a travel insurance policy before buying

A practical question list that separates appealing marketing names from what a specific policy actually covers.

By TravelReady Editorial · Published and reviewed 6 November 2026 · Educational information
The practical approach

Travel insurance is not one standardized product. Use the steps below to make a plan that fits your actual itinerary.

Begin with the risk you want to protect

Travel insurance is not one standardized product. Some coverage addresses cancellation, some medical expenses, some baggage losses and some emergency assistance. Start by identifying the financial problem you could not comfortably absorb. A traveler with expensive nonrefundable bookings may focus on different risks than someone taking a simple local weekend trip. List those risks first, and then see whether the policy genuinely responds to them.

Compare covered events, not headlines

A policy labeled “premium” may still exclude the reason a trip is interrupted. Read the relevant definition of a covered event, any required documentation and specific exclusions. Distinguish changing your mind from circumstances that might qualify for reimbursement. Check whether the policy has limits per person, per item or per incident. A benefit table alone rarely explains enough to compare two policies accurately.

Understand excesses, limits and timing

An excess or deductible can leave you responsible for part of an eligible claim. Some benefits have waiting periods, purchase deadlines, sublimits or conditions tied to when you booked travel. Compare total premium with realistic claims scenarios rather than choosing purely on price. Read the definitions of trip start, cancellation and interruption, especially if your itinerary has separate bookings or multiple travelers.

Medical and evacuation benefits deserve care

Medical assistance and evacuation coverage can be complicated and sometimes may require advance authorization. Check where coverage applies, how emergencies are coordinated, and which contact number must be called. Do not assume your regular health insurance works abroad. If you have pre-existing conditions or planned higher-risk activities, read the relevant terms carefully and ask the insurer for clarification before buying.

Check baggage and disruption scenarios

If baggage is delayed, what evidence must you provide and which necessities are potentially reimbursable? If a flight is canceled, is the relevant reason covered? If your connection uses separate tickets, are those costs addressed? Policies vary. Document the question in everyday language, then find the exact policy clause that answers it. If the answer is not clear, ask the provider in writing rather than relying on a comparison website summary.

Keep the policy useful during travel

Store the policy number, assistance contact details, claim procedure and emergency instructions offline when possible. Share the relevant assistance details with an appropriate companion while protecting sensitive information. If an incident occurs, follow emergency safety needs first, then the policy’s notice requirements when reasonably possible. Retain documents and receipts. This page is educational only; it is not individualized insurance or financial advice.

Your action checklist

  • Name the risk and find the matching covered-event clause.
  • Compare exclusions, limits and deductibles.
  • Save emergency assistance and claim instructions offline.

Verification and further reading

Travel conditions, airline terms, health recommendations and government rules may change. Confirm the exact requirements for your nationality, route and circumstances rather than treating this article as a live eligibility check.
